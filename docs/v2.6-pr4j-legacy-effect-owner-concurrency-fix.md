## P1 and corrected owner rule

The blocked PR4J owner checked `_next_step` and advanced it without
synchronization. Two threads could both observe step zero before either wrote
the cursor, causing two identical Windows-directory-claim effects while the
cursor ended at one.

Each raw owner now receives one module-created, owner-bound private state. Its
non-reentrant lock covers owner and plan replay, terminal-state and exact-step
validation, cursor advance, fixed command construction, the sole `run(...)`
statement, and observation creation. A second same-step call can reach the
runner zero times after the first call, so the pair reaches it at most once;
consecutive steps cannot reorder or overlap. Different owners have different
locks.

If `run(...)` raises any `BaseException`, the exact failure observation is
preserved and the owner becomes terminal failed before its lock is released.
Retry and every later step then fail before an effect. The lock is released on
all exception paths. Direct construction, foreign or fake synchronization,
state copied from another owner, shallow/deep copy, and pickle replay all fail
closed.

## Frozen compatibility boundary

The six command vectors, stdin bytes, `check` flags and timeout values remain
the PR4J bytes. The cursor still advances before the effect. Completed-process
observations return to the unchanged legacy transaction policy at the same
locations, including hash evidence, pre-qsub reconciliation, approval/resource
replay, qsub classification, immutable receipt publication and final ledger

The historical PR4I and PR4J fixtures remain byte-for-byte frozen. The additive
